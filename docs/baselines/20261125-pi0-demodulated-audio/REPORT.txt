Two-radio demodulated-audio comparison and IF/LO coupling control
25 November 2026 — conducted HiF link, 430.1 MHz

The principal finding is an unwanted, modulated IQ component associated with
the radios' common conversion-frequency arrangement. Moving only the TX
frequency plan from a 2495 MHz IF to a 2385 MHz IF strongly suppressed the
whistle while retaining the requested 430.1 MHz RF signal. Returning to the
standard plan restored the impairment. This is strong evidence for unwanted
coupling involving the two radios' IF/LO paths. The physical coupling route
and the particular component are not established by these measurements.

The test used the operator-confirmed nominal 90 dB attenuated HiF link,
requested/reported 0 dBm, 1 MS/s TX/RX, NBFM, and a 1 kHz synthetic tone of
amplitude 0.4 with deviation setting 2500 Hz. The actual RF power/attenuation
and reference frequencies were not independently calibrated. CTCSS/CDCSS
were disabled. The compressor is microphone-only and does not process the
synthetic source. RX used matched gain with am=-7, usb=0 and lsb=0, volume 0,
50 us de-emphasis and disabled carrier/noise squelch. RX LED1 was held low;
original LED states were restored. The same H650e headset was moved to the
receiving Pi for each direction. All workers obtained SCHED_FIFO scheduling.

Pi0a is the original board, UUID c132d0e7-d7dd-457a-b105-4a5cd97e7e80.
Pi0b is repaired device #2, UUID 36b67ac4-b313-4369-99bd-4b3e09caabfa.
The installed FPGA image was reloaded at the documented session boundaries;
the standard reference output remained 32 MHz / 8 mA (RF_CLKO=0x1A).

The 3000–3800 Hz raw-audio power fell by 24.1 dB for the clean carrier and
26.6 dB for the tone when changing A to B. The strong narrow component and
its harmonic returned in the final A stage. Both stages A and B retained
the wanted RF carrier and modulation. RX tuning registers were identical
throughout; TX tuning registers changed for B and returned to A's values.
The IF probe recorded selected 2385000000 Hz / SDK actual 2384999680 Hz.

The IF intervention was tested in this direction only. It was an isolated,
process-local diagnostic, not an installed application feature or permanent
fix. The standard IF plan was restored before final release.

Main reciprocal recordings and estimator checks

Five eight-second captures per direction recorded no-TX noise, clean carrier,
two 1 kHz tones and a final clean carrier. Representative first-tone results:

Receiver/link             Fixed notch  Tracked notch  Centered IQ replay***
Original RX, #2 TX           7.296 dB      7.313 dB          7.325 dB
Repaired #2 RX, original TX  9.268 dB      9.298 dB          9.306 dB

*** Production DSP replay after digitally centering the same captured IQ,
with a notch centered on the recovered tone. These are combined processing
changes, so the full live-to-centered difference is not a pure CFO effect.
Compare replay_tracked_notch_sinad_db with centered_replay_tracked_notch_sinad_db
in analysis/summary.json to isolate carrier centering: less than 0.012 dB for
these two captures. No physical RF frequency retuning is involved in that check.

The dominant unwanted audio tone was around 3.3–3.4 kHz. It exists in the
raw received IQ, the live raw discriminator tap, production offline replay,
independent float64 FM demodulation and accepted playback PCM. Thus it is
not explained by the playback stage or by a misleading live meter alone.

The live test tone was approximately 999.500–999.502 Hz. The recorded audio
rate correction was +500 ppm during the capture windows. Unclocked production
IQ replay recovered approximately 1000.000–1000.002 Hz. This agrees with the
resampler correction, rather than a multi-hertz error in the transmitted tone.
Tracking the tone changes the shared-IF SINAD by only 0.017–0.030 dB. After
the whistle is suppressed in stage B, tracking improves 25.179 to 26.715 dB,
so the fixed notch does affect higher-quality readings.

The estimator uses a second-order 3000 Hz low-pass, not a hard spectral cutoff.
About 99% of its residual power in the original shared-IF tone captures lies
above 3 kHz. Restricting spectrum integration to 300–3000 Hz gives diagnostic
ratios of approximately 28–29 dB for both directions. These restricted-band
figures are a separate diagnostic, not replacement standardized SINAD values.
The audible narrow signal remains a real impairment. Fitted second/third
1 kHz harmonics were relatively small; the dominant tone is nonharmonic.

Localization and modulation evidence

With pi0a TX held at 430.1 MHz and pi0b RX moved by +/-5 kHz, the unwanted
IQ line remained near +3.18 kHz while the wanted carrier moved by 10 kHz:

RX offset    Wanted IQ carrier    Unwanted IQ bin*    Strongest raw audio
+5 kHz          -5226 Hz              +3174 Hz              8395 Hz
-5 kHz          +4775 Hz              +3174 Hz              1588 Hz

* This summary uses the retained 15.26 Hz IQ peak-bin resolution. Line drift,
different averaging and peak-bin selection explain small discrepancies from
an exact carrier-to-line difference. The main localization is the roughly
10 kHz wanted-carrier shift with a stationary unwanted component. The line
is receiver-relative, but that alone cannot prove it is generated inside RX:
an external signal coupling directly into the IF can have this behavior.

Isolating the wanted and unwanted IQ components in the four original tone
captures found the same 1 kHz modulation, with fitted modulation-amplitude
ratios 0.996–1.002 and phase differences below 0.15 degree. This strongly
connects the unwanted component to the transmitted signal. Isolation keeps
only the carrier/first sidebands; its amplitudes are not deviation calibration.
See analysis/modulation-correlation.json for the method's measured outputs.

Reciprocal frequency shifts provide another clue. In the first-tone pair,
half the wanted-carrier separation is 496.75 Hz at 430.1 MHz, or 1.155 ppm.
Half the unwanted-line separation is 2876.28 Hz at 2495 MHz, or 1.153 ppm.
The second-tone pair similarly gives 1.165 / 1.165 ppm. This scaling is
consistent with a signal tied to the modem IF. A roughly 1.15 ppm reference
difference at a 2925.1 MHz LO corresponds to an audible beat near 3.36 kHz.
These are inferences from nominal sample rates, not absolute metrology or a
new RSP2pro frequency measurement. IF/LO coupling is the leading explanation;
shielding/separation, port termination or an independent RF source would
help determine the actual route. No enclosure or antenna-isolation test ran.

The no-TX captures did not show the strong corresponding IQ component. Clean
repeater listening and earlier RSP2pro observations can fit an interaction
specific to this two-CaribouLite bench setup. That interpretation remains
conditional. These data do not establish receiver sensitivity, reject every
hardware defect, or supersede the separately recorded 23 November sweeps.

Physical-layout follow-up and working conclusion

A subsequent repeat with increased physical separation and one Pi rotated
90 degrees, retaining the same nominal 90 dB attenuated HiF link and standard
IF plan, improved live SINAD from 9.29/9.28 to 24.59/24.55 dB. Power in the
original 3000–3800 Hz whistle band fell about 25 dB. This supports coupling
in the two-radio bench arrangement as the explanation for the whistle.
We are no longer pursuing an intrinsic physical radio defect for this
impairment; separate hardware-test findings remain independent. See
../20261125-pi0-physical-separation/REPORT.txt for the evidence and limits.

Capture, analysis, verification and reproduction

Eighteen captures retain 144 million decoded IQ samples and 6.912 million
frames for each of three audio taps: raw pre-volume/pre-de-emphasis float32,
post-filter/pre-gain float32 and accepted ALSA playback PCM. Raw captures are
eight seconds at nominal 1 MS/s / 48 kHz. The WAV exports preserve playback
samples and gain without normalization. One fresh production replay can end
one sample short at its fractional phase boundary; no padding is introduced.

Bounded buffers were prefaulted and recorded in RAM, then written after
streaming shutdown. Actual production DSP objects were reused in isolated
diagnostic backends. Additional code forwards IQ/audio/PCM unchanged and
permits zero-amplitude synthetic tone. Native mock tests checked forwarding,
signal arming, capture limits, partial ALSA writes and correction metadata.
The optional IF probe was separately tested for scoped override, unchanged
other channels/frequencies, return propagation and invalid-input rejection.
Installed applications, shared libraries, firmware files and flash were not
modified. Both radios were independently confirmed released after all tests.

All capture-window queue/frame/playback/driver error deltas and clipping
counts were zero. All starts succeeded without retries. Recording completeness,
role/settings checks, applied scheduling, metadata and release checks pass.
Each raw capture file has a retained SHA-256; top-level SHA256SUMS protects
the complete package. Verification is offline and never transmits RF.

Plots: analysis/direction-comparison.png; analysis/receiver-offset-localization.png;
analysis/tx-if-aba-comparison.png. Per-case spectra and analysis/summary.json
retain complete results. The *-host.tar.gz archives retain the raw recordings,
status histories, commands, metadata, build identities and transmitter logs.
